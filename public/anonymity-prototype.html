UUnask
Anonymity upgrade proposal

01 · The opportunity

Move from anonymous records to stronger anonymity.

Today, Unask does not save an employee’s name or email with a question. However, the authenticated submission request can still be correlated through platform access logs.

1Employee signs inGoogle confirms they are an approved employee.
2Session is createdThe browser receives an authenticated session.
3Question is sentThe authenticated request goes to the backend.
4Identity is omittedNo author field is stored with the question.
5Logs remainPrivileged operators may correlate request and login evidence.
△Current protection: HR cannot see an author in Unask, but a highly privileged platform administrator may still correlate technical logs.

02 · Recommended model

Verify eligibility without attaching identity to the question.

A blind privacy pass separates “this is an approved employee” from “this employee submitted this question.”

1Verify onceGoogle SSO confirms employee eligibility.
2Issue blind passThe verifier signs a hidden, one-time token.
3Unlink the passThe browser reveals a valid token the issuer never saw.
4Submit separatelyThe question is sent without the employee’s login session.
5Reduce metadataMinimal logs plus delayed or batched writes reduce timing clues.
✓Key result: the eligibility service knows who received a pass, while the question service sees a valid pass but not who received it.

03 · What changes for HR

The moderation workflow stays familiar. The privacy promise gets stronger.

HR can still

  • Review, approve, hide or delete questions
  • Assign questions to the right responder
  • Ask private follow-up questions through a recovery code
  • See aggregate trends and response performance

HR cannot

  • See an employee name, email or user ID on a question
  • Use the privacy pass to identify its recipient
  • Search for everything submitted by one employee
  • Recover identity from the encrypted recovery vault
iNo system can promise zero inference. Small groups, writing style and voluntary details in the message can still reveal identity. The product should state this clearly.

04 · Approval requested

Approve a technical proof of concept—not a production launch.

The first build will validate the privacy design, usability and operational controls before any employee rollout.

Proposed proof-of-concept scope

  • Blind one-time privacy pass issuance and redemption
  • Separate identity verification from question submission
  • Minimal logging and timing-correlation controls
  • Security, privacy and legal review before pilot use
Decision needed

May we proceed with designing and testing this stronger-anonymity proof of concept?
